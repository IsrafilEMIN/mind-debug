# Worked anchor: serving SMBs with applied AI

This is an illustrative decision, not market research or a claim that a particular niche will pay. All example timeboxes and acceptance bars below are proposals to negotiate, not observed results.

## Situation

A technically curious builder wants to deliver useful applied AI to SMBs. No accessible customer, validated workflow, or baseline has yet been established. They are considering kernel/inference study, building a local LLM system, or learning n8n.

**Direction:** build the ability to diagnose and deliver reliable business improvements using appropriate engineering.

**Next milestone:** establish an accessible, recurring workflow with an accountable owner, measurable pain, authorized data access, and a credible reason to test a change. This is evidence for a pilot—not proof of demand or willingness to pay.

**Bottleneck hypothesis:** uncertainty about a valuable, reachable problem precedes uncertainty about the inference stack. Disproof: an existing client already has validated demand, representative data, and a documented deployment blocker.

## Candidate routes

| Route | Why it might help | Why it is or is not Now |
|---|---|---|
| Learn kernels/build local inference | Deep systems capability; offline/private serving | Does not establish a workflow or owner. Defer unless a verified deployment requirement makes it necessary. |
| Complete a broad n8n course | Integration familiarity | Tool competence does not establish demand or safe delivery. Learn the relevant integration just in time if selected. |
| Inspect an accessible business workflow with its operator | Tests whether the problem, access, and baseline exist | Preferred under the stated unknowns: inexpensive evidence that can change the build decision. |
| Build a generic AI-agent demo | May help explain an offer | Weak evidence about real workflow value. Prefer a narrow prototype only when it resolves a named uncertainty. |
| Manual/process-only improvement | Can expose whether simpler changes suffice | A genuine contender, not a token comparison. If it solves the problem adequately, do not force AI into it. |

## Proposed priority brief

**Now:** run one bounded workflow-diagnosis cycle with a reachable operator. The user is the proposed owner; confirm their access and availability first.

- **First step:** identify a reachable business/operator and request permission for a workflow walkthrough. Drafting or sending outreach requires the user's authorization; this skill itself sends nothing.
- **Timebox:** propose one week, adjusted to actual access. If nobody is reachable, the immediate blocker is customer access; test an outreach route rather than inventing interview findings.
- **Artifact:** a redacted current-state workflow map and baseline note: frequency, handling time/cost, error/rework rate, missed opportunities, systems involved, decision points, data availability, and who authorizes change. Unknowns remain unknown.
- **Acceptance:** the operator confirms the map, a consequential recurring problem, and whether an authorized test is feasible. Record evidence about ownership and budget; do not equate an enthusiastic conversation with a purchase commitment.
- **Stop/pivot:** if pain is minor, inaccessible, unmeasurable, or already cheaply solved, do not build this pilot. Choose another workflow or close the hypothesis.
- **Review:** at the walkthrough or timebox end, decide whether evidence supports a scoped feasibility test. No evidence means no claim of validation.

**Defer:** kernel tuning, hardware buying, generic agent frameworks, broad automation courses. Revisit only when a specific requirement or measured bottleneck creates a causal need. Personal-interest study is allowed as explicitly separate time, not disguised customer progress.

## Conditional route after discovery

1. **Diagnose the intervention.** Compare process repair, conventional software, deterministic automation, an AI-assisted workflow, human judgment, and no change. Language variability may justify AI; exact rules may favor ordinary code. Keep consequential approvals with an authorized human when risk warrants it.
2. **Define the smallest end-to-end slice.** For example, draft a structured intake record from an authorized sample, validate required fields, route ambiguity to a reviewer, and export only after approval. This is a hypothetical slice, not the selected business opportunity.
3. **Test feasibility before promises.** Agree representative cases, edge cases, acceptable errors, reviewer load, latency, and cost thresholds. Compare with the current workflow and a simpler alternative. Include failures, not just happy-path demos.
4. **Pilot safely.** Use least-privilege access, approved data handling/retention, bounded actions, timeout/retry and duplicate-action protection where relevant, audit logs, human escalation, rollback/manual recovery, and a named operator. Move from shadow mode to live actions only after explicit approval and acceptance checks.
5. **Verify business value.** Measure time/cost saved or revenue improvement after review effort, failures, operating costs, and support. Use a baseline and agreed observation window; do not infer causal revenue gains from a handful of coincident sales.
6. **Production decision.** Proceed only with an accountable owner, accepted quality thresholds, monitoring, incident/recovery arrangements, and evidence the improvement survives realistic use. Production-level means controlled and tested for this risk context—not bug-free.

## When the recommendation must reverse

- **Verified offline requirement:** local serving can become a prerequisite. First test whether an existing supported local runtime meets requirements; kernel engineering still requires a measured need.
- **Existing validated customer, unsafe prototype:** reliability/evaluation and safe delivery become Now; more general customer interviews may be avoidance.
- **Evidence says deterministic automation suffices:** use it, possibly with n8n. If the user wants an AI-native offer, seek a genuinely AI-dependent problem instead of mislabeling this one.
- **Goal is inference-engine research:** kernels may be directly aligned. Do not optimize that user for SMB revenue.
- **Uncertain value and costly implementation:** a manual or concierge test may be the fastest evidence-generating route, provided data use and human effort are honest and authorized.
