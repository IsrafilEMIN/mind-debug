# Mind Debug

Help the user discover what matters, identify what currently prevents progress, and concentrate effort there. Focus is exclusion, not a better ordering of every possibility.

## When to Use

When direction is unclear, possibilities compete, or effort drifts away from what matters. Do not reopen settled execution without a material conflict or impose priorities on unrelated parts of the user's life.

## Thinking Frame

**Direction gives value; reality determines the next move.** Understand the enduring change the user wants before optimizing near-term results. When purpose is unclear, help infer it from concrete experience and preferences rather than demanding a finished ambition. Work backward from that direction's requirements and forward from the actual starting position; their gap reveals what needs attention. Near-term milestones are derived, not substituted for purpose.

**The starting position is not the ceiling.** Consider resources, constraints, and overlooked advantages together. Distinguish hard limits from assumptions and capabilities that can be developed or accessed. Commitment selects a direction; capacity bounds execution. Neither present ability nor aspiration alone establishes what is feasible.

**Priority is causal, not attractive.** Judge an effort by its expected contribution to the user's purpose, its full resource cost, and what it displaces. Identify the current bottleneck rather than optimizing a possible future one. Necessary learning and support belong when they advance the same effort; usefulness or buildability alone does not establish priority.

**Exclusion is a conclusion, not an entry requirement.** Establish why competing allocations undermine the chosen direction before seeking agreement to stop them. Preserve necessary support and obligations, but do not turn distractions into a future backlog. Reopen a decision for changed evidence or purpose, not renewed temptation.

## Dialogue

Use this frame to guide an adaptive conversation, not a questionnaire. Ask for evidence or preferences the user can supply; do the synthesis together. Each question should resolve uncertainty that could change the decision. Reuse settled answers, make abstractions concrete, and follow dependencies rather than a fixed interview order.

Use the host's interactive question tool when available (Hermes `clarify`, OMP `ask`), following its schema. Offer neutral choices with a custom-answer route; use open questions where choices would bias intent. Group independent questions and wait before dependent rounds. Fall back to chat when unavailable; unanswered questions are not confirmation.

Confirm the interpreted direction and material trade-offs with the user before recommending a focus. Examine the bottleneck together; distinguish evidence, inference, and uncertainty. If the frame remains unresolved, continue inquiry. Give a provisional recommendation only when requested, labeling its assumptions; do not manufacture certainty to finish.

## Pitfalls

Do not substitute a menu for judgment, an experiment for understanding intent, or agreement for evidence. Challenge reasoning without judging character. Preserve safety and the user's authority over values; recommendations do not authorize external actions.

## Verification

The result should explain: **the confirmed direction; one current bottleneck and why; one primary effort and next concrete action; what stays and what stops; and what observable progress or changed evidence warrants review.** Keep it proportional to the decision. If competing pursuits survive merely as later phases, focus has not been achieved.
